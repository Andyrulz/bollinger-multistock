# Swing Screener — Chart Case Studies

**Purpose:** Record practical chart examples that can inform future scanner research and validation.  
**Scope:** Observational studies only; these are not automated trade instructions or evidence of future returns.

## Case Study 1 — Enviro Infra Engineers Limited

**Exchange:** NSE  
**Timeframe:** Daily  
**Chart date:** 2026-07-20  
**Chart close:** ₹229.35  
**Daily range:** ₹218.15–₹231.50  
**Daily change:** +4.14%  
**Displayed ADR:** 5.12%

### 1. Setup summary

Enviro Infra Engineers is an emerging recovery and momentum setup. It combines a strong advance from a recent swing low, exceptional accumulation-volume events, multiple upside fair-value gaps, bullish short-term moving-average alignment, a reclaim of the 200-day moving average, and tight price action near the rising 10-day moving average.

The main attraction is not simply that the stock has risen. The constructive feature is that price has retained much of its advance while consolidating near the short-term average, potentially creating a compact pivot with definable risk.

### 2. Proximity to the 52-week high

The visible major high/resistance is approximately ₹305. At the ₹229.35 close, the stock is about 24.8% below that level:

$$
\frac{305 - 229.35}{305} \times 100 \approx 24.8\%
$$

This places it within approximately 25%–30% of its 52-week high. That is constructive because the stock has recovered a substantial portion of its prior decline, but it also means historical overhead supply remains between the current price and ₹305.

### 3. Exceptional accumulation volume

The chart marks a highest-volume-ever event around 13 April, with volume of approximately 85.66 million shares. Several additional large bullish volume bars appeared after that event.

Constructive characteristics include:

- The exceptional volume arrived while price was advancing out of the March–April base.
- The largest bars were associated predominantly with upside expansion rather than breakdowns.
- Large bullish-volume sessions continued after the initial expansion, suggesting repeated demand rather than a single isolated event.
- Price retained a large part of the volume-backed advance instead of immediately returning to the base.

The latest session shows approximately 3.23 million shares against displayed average volume near 2.46 million. This is positive, although materially smaller than the earlier institutional-volume events.

### 4. Three consecutive upside fair-value gaps

The chart identifies three upside fair-value-gap zones created during the recovery from the March low:

1. Lower zone around ₹146–155.
2. Middle zone around ₹174–198.
3. Upper zone around ₹198–210.

These gaps represent rapid upside repricing and price imbalance. Their constructive features are:

- All three formed while price moved away from the base or subsequent higher-low structures.
- No corresponding downside gap has appeared to negate the bullish sequence.
- The lower gaps remain well below the current market price.
- The upper gap overlaps an important moving-average and demand area, potentially creating support around ₹198–210.

An unfilled gap is not automatically support. Its value depends on how price behaves when revisiting the zone. A decisive high-volume close through the upper zone would weaken this part of the thesis.

### 5. Moving-average structure

The chart shows:

- Price above the 200-day moving average.
- The 10-day moving average above the 20-day moving average.
- The 20-day moving average above the 50-day moving average.
- The 10-, 20-, and 50-day averages all rising.

This is bullish short-term alignment:

$$
\text{Price} > \text{SMA}_{10} > \text{SMA}_{20} > \text{SMA}_{50}
$$

The 200-day average is below price, around the ₹200 area, but still appears relatively flat or slightly declining. Therefore, this is an early long-term trend recovery rather than a fully mature Stage Two structure with a strongly rising 200-day average.

### 6. Advance from the nearest swing low

The nearest meaningful swing low is approximately ₹175. From ₹175 to the ₹229.35 close, the stock gained about 31.1%:

$$
\frac{229.35 - 175}{175} \times 100 \approx 31.1\%
$$

The recent swing high near ₹245 represents an advance of approximately 40% from ₹175:

$$
\frac{245 - 175}{175} \times 100 = 40\%
$$

This satisfies the observed 30%–40% prior-impulse characteristic. A meaningful prior impulse is useful because a tight consolidation after a strong move can represent temporary supply absorption rather than a lack of demand.

### 7. Tightness near the 10-day moving average

Following the advance toward ₹240–245, price pulled back toward the rising 10-day moving average and began holding in a relatively compact area around ₹218–230.

Constructive observations:

- The pullback is occurring above the 20- and 50-day moving averages.
- Price has not retraced deeply into the prior ₹175–210 consolidation.
- The latest candle recovered strongly from ₹218.15 and closed at ₹229.35, near its session high of ₹231.50.
- The rising 10-day average provides a nearby technical reference for risk control.
- Tightness near a rising short-term average can form a pivot if volume contracts during consolidation and expands on the eventual breakout.

This is the principal low-risk-entry characteristic visible on the chart. A compact pivot can permit a stop below the tight area rather than below the entire recovery base.

### 8. Potential pivot and confirmation

Two pivot interpretations are visible:

#### Early pivot

A move through approximately ₹231.50–₹233 would clear the latest candle and immediate tight area.

This is the more aggressive entry because resistance remains near ₹240–245. It is only attractive when the tight-area low allows sufficiently compact risk.

#### Conventional resistance pivot

A decisive close through approximately ₹245 would clear the recent July swing high.

Preferred confirmation would include:

- Expanding volume relative to recent sessions.
- A strong close near the daily high.
- No immediate close back below the pivot.
- Continued bullish alignment of the 10/20/50-day averages.

A low-volume retest that holds above the breakout zone could offer better entry efficiency than chasing a wide breakout candle.

### 9. Risk/reward framework

The exact trade plan depends on the selected pivot and the final tight-area low.

#### Aggressive tight-area framework

- Trigger: above approximately ₹231.50–₹233.
- Invalidation reference: below approximately ₹217–218 or the confirmed tight-area low.
- Immediate resistance: ₹240–245.
- Subsequent resistance: ₹255–265.
- Longer-range resistance: ₹280 and ₹300–305.

This approach offers nearby structural invalidation but limited room to the first resistance. It therefore requires strong evidence that ₹240–245 can be absorbed.

#### Confirmed breakout framework

- Trigger: decisive breakout above approximately ₹245.
- Invalidation reference: failed breakout or loss of the breakout/retest low.
- Initial objective area: ₹260–265.
- Secondary area: ₹280.
- Major historical resistance: ₹300–305.

A breakout should not be accepted merely because price trades briefly above ₹245. Volume, closing location, and follow-through are important.

### 10. Constructive characteristics checklist

| Characteristic                            | Observation                                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Within 25%–30% of 52-week high            | Yes; approximately 24.8% below ₹305                                                        |
| Significant prior impulse                 | Yes; approximately 31% to the close and 40% to the recent high from ₹175                   |
| Exceptional institutional-volume evidence | Yes; chart marks highest-ever volume around 13 April, followed by other large bullish bars |
| Consecutive upside fair-value gaps        | Yes; three visible zones from the recovery base                                            |
| Downside gap negating the sequence        | None visible on the supplied chart                                                         |
| Above 200-day moving average              | Yes                                                                                        |
| Bullish 10/20/50-day alignment            | Yes                                                                                        |
| Short moving averages rising              | Yes                                                                                        |
| Tightness near rising 10-day average      | Yes                                                                                        |
| Compact pivot developing                  | Yes, approximately ₹231.50–₹245 depending on entry style                                   |
| Nearby overhead resistance                | Yes; ₹240–245 and ₹255–265                                                                 |
| Mature rising 200-day trend               | Not yet clearly established                                                                |

### 11. What makes it a useful swing candidate

The combination is notable:

1. **Price strength:** It has advanced materially from the nearest swing low and is within approximately 25% of its major high.
2. **Demand evidence:** Exceptional and repeated bullish volume appeared during the recovery.
3. **Persistent imbalance:** Three upside fair-value gaps remain visible without a downside-gap sequence.
4. **Trend improvement:** Price reclaimed the 200-day average while the shorter averages achieved bullish alignment.
5. **Constructive digestion:** The stock is consolidating near the rising 10-day average rather than fully retracing the impulse.
6. **Defined pivot:** The tight area creates identifiable trigger and invalidation levels.
7. **Adequate volatility:** ADR around 5.12% provides swing potential, although it also requires disciplined sizing.

### 12. Risks and disconfirming evidence

- Price remains below the recent ₹240–245 swing high.
- Historical supply exists around ₹255–265, ₹280, and ₹300–305.
- The 200-day average is not yet clearly rising.
- Current volume is positive but not comparable to April’s exceptional volume.
- The chart’s quarterly table shows mixed earnings growth, including negative recent EPS comparisons.
- A close below approximately ₹217–218 would damage the immediate tight pivot.
- A decisive loss of ₹198–210 would invalidate the upper fair-value-gap support and moving-average-reclaim thesis.
- A break below ₹175 would invalidate the higher-low recovery structure.

### 13. Scanner research implications

This case suggests several features worth measuring or surfacing in scanner research:

- Distance from 52-week high within a configurable 25%–30% range.
- Highest-ever or percentile-ranked volume events in the prior 3–6 months.
- Count and direction of recent fair-value gaps.
- Whether bullish gaps remain open or defended.
- Price above the 200-day moving average.
- Bullish and rising 10/20/50-day moving-average alignment.
- A prior 30%–40% impulse from a recent structural low.
- Tightness around the rising 10-day average.
- Pivot distance and structural stop width.
- Overhead resistance and available reward measured in units of risk.

These are research observations only. Adding any of them as automated scanner gates requires a separate specification, implementation plan, backtest, and quality-control process.

### 14. Case-study conclusion

Enviro Infra Engineers is a constructive swing candidate because it combines strong prior momentum, unusual accumulation volume, persistent upside imbalances, improving trend alignment, and a developing tight pivot near the rising 10-day moving average.

Its quality comes from the **combination** of those features rather than any single indicator. The main unresolved issue is overhead supply around ₹240–245. The chart becomes more actionable if price either:

- Clears that resistance with decisive volume and follow-through, or
- Continues contracting near the 10-day average and creates an even tighter, clearly invalidated pivot.

The setup should be treated as a watchlist and entry-planning case study, not as an instruction to buy.

## Case Study 2 — Sansera Engineering Limited

**Exchange:** NSE  
**Timeframe:** Daily  
**Setup-snapshot close:** ₹1,424.70  
**Outcome-snapshot close:** ₹3,335.20  
**Outcome-snapshot high:** ₹3,457.50  
**Setup-snapshot ADR:** 3.19%  
**Outcome-snapshot ADR:** 3.89%

### 1. Purpose of this before-and-after study

The first Sansera snapshot showed a low-risk continuation setup before its major advance. The later snapshot shows what happened after the stock cleared the identified ₹1,450–1,465 pivot and ₹1,510–1,525 resistance.

The purpose is not to select a winner with hindsight. It is to isolate the characteristics that were observable at the original decision point and distinguish them from confirmation that appeared only after the breakout.

### 2. Original setup

At ₹1,424.70, Sansera had already completed several constructive phases:

1. A major recovery from approximately ₹960–980.
2. A strong advance toward ₹1,390.
3. A multi-month consolidation.
4. An August shakeout toward approximately ₹1,220.
5. A rapid reclaim of the moving averages.
6. A volume-backed advance toward ₹1,520.
7. A controlled consolidation around ₹1,380–1,460.

This created a potential continuation shelf or handle immediately below resistance.

### 3. Observable low-risk-entry characteristics

#### Strong prior impulse

From the more recent ₹1,220 swing low to the ₹1,520 swing high, the stock advanced approximately 24.6%:

$$
\frac{1520 - 1220}{1220} \times 100 \approx 24.6\%
$$

The advance demonstrated demand before the tight setup formed.

#### Volume expansion followed by contraction

The move toward ₹1,520 contained several large bullish-volume sessions, including a chart-marked high-volume event around 690,000 shares. Volume then contracted materially while price consolidated near the short-term averages.

This sequence was constructive:

- Expansion volume confirmed the prior thrust.
- Price retained most of the advance.
- Lower consolidation volume suggested reduced selling pressure.
- The stock formed a tighter structure instead of immediately retracing the impulse.

#### Improving moving-average structure

At the setup snapshot:

- Price was above the 50-day moving average.
- Price was above the 200-day moving average.
- The 50-day average was rising.
- The short averages had compressed beneath price and were positioned to turn upward again.

The 200-day average was still relatively flat or slightly declining, so the setup was an improving trend rather than a perfect mature Stage Two structure.

#### Tightness near the short-term averages

The stock held approximately ₹1,380–1,460 after reaching ₹1,520. This produced a nearby structural reference for invalidation and avoided requiring a stop beneath the entire ₹1,220 recovery leg.

The combination of tight price ranges, quieter volume, and support near the short averages was the principal low-risk-entry feature.

#### Resistance-to-support behavior

The ₹1,440–1,455 region had acted as prior resistance. Price broke through it during the initial advance and then consolidated around that area. A successful reclaim and hold could convert old resistance into support.

#### Improving earnings

The setup snapshot showed improving EPS comparisons, including approximately +41% in the latest displayed quarter, while sales growth was positive but more modest. This provided fundamental support for the technical recovery.

### 4. Original pivot and risk framework

Two trigger levels were identifiable before the subsequent advance:

#### Early pivot

- Trigger area: approximately ₹1,450–1,465.
- Tight-structure invalidation: approximately ₹1,375–1,390.
- First resistance: approximately ₹1,510–1,525.

Using ₹1,455 as an illustrative trigger and ₹1,380 as the structural stop:

$$
	ext{Initial structural risk} = \frac{1455 - 1380}{1455} \times 100 \approx 5.2\%
$$

The first resistance offered less than 1R, so the setup required evidence that ₹1,520 could eventually be cleared.

#### Confirmation pivot

A decisive close above ₹1,510–1,525 provided stronger confirmation that the larger continuation had begun. This reduced false-breakout risk but required a different stop or smaller position because entry was farther from the original tight-area low.

### 5. What happened afterward

The outcome snapshot shows that Sansera:

1. Cleared the ₹1,450–1,465 pivot.
2. Cleared the ₹1,510–1,525 swing high.
3. Converted the broad ₹1,400 area from resistance into long-term support.
4. Advanced through approximately ₹1,700, ₹1,800, ₹2,200, ₹2,500, ₹2,800, and ₹3,200.
5. Closed the outcome snapshot at ₹3,335.20 after reaching ₹3,457.50.

From an illustrative ₹1,455 pivot to ₹3,335.20, the gain was approximately 129.2%:

$$
\frac{3335.20 - 1455}{1455} \times 100 \approx 129.2\%
$$

From the same pivot to the displayed ₹3,457.50 high, the maximum visible advance was approximately 137.6%:

$$
\frac{3457.50 - 1455}{1455} \times 100 \approx 137.6\%
$$

The stock more than doubled after the low-risk setup. That outcome validates the usefulness of the original characteristics as a research example, but it does not imply that every similar setup will produce a comparable move.

### 6. Post-breakout price behavior

#### Stair-step trend

The stock did not move directly from ₹1,455 to ₹3,335. It advanced through repeated cycles:

1. Breakout or thrust.
2. Short consolidation or pullback.
3. Support near the rising short-term averages.
4. Renewed breakout.

This stair-step behavior is characteristic of a sustained institutional trend. It also provided multiple opportunities to trail risk without requiring prediction of the final target.

#### Multiple upside fair-value gaps

The outcome chart shows several upside fair-value-gap zones during the advance, particularly around approximately:

- ₹1,700–1,800.
- ₹1,850–2,000.
- ₹2,400–2,750.

The gaps represent rapid repricing. Price generally held above or found support around these zones, reinforcing the strength of the trend.

#### Mature moving-average alignment

By the outcome snapshot, the trend had developed into a much stronger configuration:

$$
	ext{Price} > \text{SMA}_{10} > \text{SMA}_{20} > \text{SMA}_{50} > \text{SMA}_{200}
$$

The 200-day moving average had turned decisively upward. This was important post-entry confirmation that the original recovery had matured into a sustained Stage Two advance.

#### Repeated accumulation-volume events

The later chart contains repeated large bullish-volume bars, including a highest-volume-year event of approximately 3.04 million shares. These events occurred during price expansion rather than sustained breakdown.

The sequence demonstrates that the first volume-backed thrust was not isolated; institutional demand continued as price moved through higher levels.

#### Earnings acceleration

The outcome snapshot shows stronger fundamental comparisons than the original setup:

- September EPS growth: approximately +41%.
- December EPS growth: approximately +23%.
- March EPS growth: approximately +104%.
- December sales growth: approximately +25%.
- March sales growth: approximately +28%.

The later earnings acceleration supported the sustained technical trend. Much of this information was unavailable at the original entry point and must therefore be treated as post-entry confirmation rather than an original selection criterion.

### 7. Why the original entry was low risk

The original setup was attractive because risk could be defined before the outcome was known:

- Price had already demonstrated a strong prior impulse.
- The pullback retained most of the impulse.
- Volume contracted during consolidation.
- Price held above the major averages.
- The 50-day average was rising.
- The consolidation formed close to the 10/20-day averages.
- The ₹1,450–1,465 pivot was clearly visible.
- The ₹1,375–1,390 tight-area low supplied a structural invalidation point.
- Improving EPS provided fundamental confirmation.

The correct lesson is not that Sansera eventually traded above ₹3,300. The lesson is that a potentially large trend was available through an entry whose initial structural risk was approximately 5%.

### 8. Entry detection versus trend holding

Finding the low-risk pivot and capturing the full advance are separate problems.

#### Entry detection requires

- A prior impulse.
- Tightness and volume contraction.
- A nearby pivot.
- Compact structural invalidation.
- Adequate liquidity.
- Acceptable overhead supply.

#### Trend holding requires

- Respect for rising moving averages.
- Avoiding premature exits during normal contractions.
- Monitoring distribution and failed breakouts.
- Trailing risk beneath meaningful swing lows or moving-average structures.
- Allowing repeated breakout–consolidation cycles to develop.

A scanner can identify the original setup, but a separate exit framework is required to remain in a move that doubles.

| Positive sales growth                  | Yes, though modest at the original setup       |
| Post-breakout fair-value-gap sequence  | Yes                                            |

### 10. Scanner research implications

This case suggests that the research system should eventually be able to surface combinations of:

- A 20%–40% prior impulse.
- Price within a configurable distance of a 52-week high.
- One or more unusually large bullish-volume events before consolidation.
- Volume contraction during the final tight area.
- A final tight area near the rising 10- or 20-day average.
- Price above the 50- and 200-day averages.
- Rising 50-day average and improving 10/20/50-day alignment.
- Pivot distance small enough to produce a compact stop.
- Evidence that former resistance is being reclaimed or retested.
- Improving quarterly EPS and sales growth.
- Adequate reward beyond immediate overhead resistance.

Post-breakout monitoring could separately surface:

- New upside fair-value gaps.
- Repeated high-volume expansion sessions.
- Successful tests of rising short-term averages.
- A rising 200-day moving average.
- Successive higher bases.
- Earnings acceleration.

These remain research observations. Automating them requires precise definitions, point-in-time data, backtesting, and explicit approval before scanner logic changes.

### 11. Hindsight and survivorship-bias safeguards

This outcome must not be used to retroactively make the original setup appear certain.

- At ₹1,424.70, resistance at ₹1,450–1,525 was real.
- The 200-day average was not yet strongly rising.
- The first target provided limited reward relative to the initial structural stop.
- A high-volume failure below ₹1,375 could have invalidated the setup.
- Later fair-value gaps, volume events, and earnings acceleration were not known in advance.

Future pattern research should include both successful and failed examples with the same original characteristics. Selection quality must be judged from information available on the setup date, not from the eventual chart.

### 12. Case-study conclusion

Sansera demonstrates the type of stock the Swing Screener should seek at low-risk entry points: a fundamentally improving company with a strong prior impulse, evidence of institutional demand, a controlled low-volume contraction, improving moving-average structure, and a clearly defined pivot close to structural invalidation.

After clearing the original resistance, the stock developed into a mature Stage Two trend with repeated accumulation, upside fair-value gaps, higher bases, bullish moving-average alignment, and earnings acceleration. The move from the approximate ₹1,455 pivot to ₹3,335.20 illustrates why compact-risk entries can have asymmetric potential when a genuine institutional trend follows.

The essential objective is not to predict ₹3,300 at entry. It is to identify a setup where downside can be defined near ₹1,380 while the upside remains open if resistance is absorbed and the trend confirms.

## Case Study 3 — Jammu & Kashmir Bank Limited

**Exchange:** NSE  
**Timeframe:** Daily  
**Setup-snapshot close:** ₹123.39  
**Outcome-snapshot close:** ₹182.25  
**Setup-snapshot ADR:** 4.97%  
**Outcome-snapshot ADR:** 4.38%

### 1. Setup summary

J&K Bank is an example of a long reaccumulation base followed by a breakout, low-risk retest, and sustained markup. Unlike Sansera, the displayed earnings record was mixed; the strongest evidence came from price structure, repeated support, breakout volume, and resistance-to-support conversion.

### 2. Long reaccumulation base

After advancing from approximately ₹45 to ₹150, the stock corrected and spent roughly 18–24 months absorbing supply. The broad structure contained:

- Repeated support around ₹88–100.
- Intermediate resistance around ₹108–115.
- Major historical resistance around ₹148–152.
- Several failed attempts to sustain prices below the lower part of the range.

Repeated lower-wick reversals near support suggested that sellers were being absorbed. The early-2026 rejection near ₹98–102 acted like a final shakeout before markup, although formal Wyckoff classification would require additional intraday and volume analysis.

### 3. Original low-risk setup

The key sequence was:

1. Breakout above the long-standing ₹108–115 ceiling.
2. Advance toward ₹125–128.
3. Controlled pullback toward approximately ₹112–116.
4. Hold above the reclaimed breakout zone and rising short averages.
5. Bullish recovery to ₹123.39, near the session high.

This formed a classic breakout–retest–higher-low continuation structure. The best risk/reward was available during the retest around ₹116–120; a continuation trigger existed around ₹124–126.

### 4. Volume and moving-average evidence

The breakout phase included exceptional bullish-volume events around 33.89 million, 29.85 million, and 11.84 million shares. Price retained the breakout rather than immediately collapsing into the base.

At the setup point:

- Price was above the 200-day moving average.
- The 200-day average was flattening after its decline.
- The 50-day average was turning upward.
- The 10- and 20-day averages were rising.
- Pullback volume was less threatening than the breakout demand.

This was an early Stage Two transition, before the later mature moving-average alignment became obvious.

### 5. Original risk/reward

An illustrative continuation framework was:

- Entry: ₹123–124.
- Structural invalidation: approximately ₹116–118.
- Major initial objective: ₹148–152.

Potential room from ₹123.39 to ₹150 was approximately 21.6%:

$$
\frac{150 - 123.39}{123.39} \times 100 \approx 21.6\%
$$

With approximately 5%–6.5% structural risk, the first major supply zone offered roughly 3R–4R. This was materially more attractive than waiting until price had already reached ₹150.

### 6. What happened afterward

The ₹108–115 breakout held, higher lows continued, and price eventually cleared the major ₹148–152 historical high. Acceptance above ₹150 removed the largest visible supply zone and moved the stock into price discovery.

From ₹123.39 to ₹182.25, the gain was approximately 47.7%:

$$
\frac{182.25 - 123.39}{123.39} \times 100 \approx 47.7\%
$$

The later visible high near ₹197 represented an approximate 59.7% maximum advance from the setup close. The outcome chart also showed mature bullish moving-average alignment, continued high-volume participation, and an upside fair-value-gap zone around ₹165–176.

### 7. Characteristics checklist

| Characteristic                      | J&K Bank evidence                     |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Long accumulation period            | Approximately 18–24 months            |
| Repeated structural support         | ₹88–100                               |
| Failed breakdown or bull wick       | Present near the final base           |
| Major range resistance              | ₹108–115                              |
| Exceptional breakout volume         | Yes                                   |
| Successful resistance retest        | Approximately ₹112–116                |
| Price above 200-day average         | Yes                                   |
| 50-day average turning upward       | Yes                                   |
| Compact structural stop             | Approximately 5%–7%                   |
| Meaningful room to next supply      | Approximately 22% to ₹150             |
| Subsequent historical-high breakout | ₹150 cleared                          |
| Mature Stage Two confirmation later | Yes                                   |
| Strong earnings acceleration        | No; displayed fundamentals were mixed |

### 8. Main lesson

This case demonstrates a technically led pattern:

> A long, well-defined base; repeated rejection of lower prices; exceptional breakout volume; a successful retest of old resistance; and a tight continuation pivot with meaningful room to the next major supply zone.

The entry was attractive because the downside was defined near the reclaimed ₹108–118 structure while the upside remained open if ₹150 was later absorbed. At ₹182, the original compact-risk opportunity had passed; a fresh entry would require a new tight base or successful breakout/retest around ₹195–200.

### 9. Scanner research implications

This case suggests measuring:

- Base duration and repeated support tests.
- Failed breakdowns or lower-wick reversals near structural support.
- Breakout through multi-month resistance.
- Exceptional bullish volume around the breakout.
- Retest proximity to former resistance.
- Improving, not necessarily already perfect, moving-average alignment.
- Compact structural risk.
- Distance to the next major historical resistance.
- Separate technical-quality and fundamental-quality scores so a strong price/volume setup with mixed earnings remains visible for research.

## Case Study 4 — VA Tech Wabag Limited

**Exchange:** NSE  
**Timeframe:** Daily  
**Current chart close:** ₹2,117  
**Displayed ADR:** 5.14%

### 1. Two-entry setup summary

Wabag demonstrates two distinct low-risk entry models within one developing institutional trend:

1. **Early trend-transition entry:** below the previous 52-week high, after recovery from the base and a pullback into the reclaimed moving-average structure.
2. **Post-high continuation entry:** after clearing the previous 52-week high, when price again tightened near the rising 10-day average with quieter volume.

The first entry offered better reward/risk but less confirmation. The second offered stronger Stage Two confirmation but required a new compact structure to avoid chasing an extended move.

### 2. Long base and failed breakdown

After an earlier advance from approximately ₹500 to ₹1,950, Wabag entered a lengthy corrective base. Important levels included:

- Major support around ₹1,050–1,150.
- Intermediate support around ₹1,350–1,450.
- Long resistance around ₹1,680–1,700.
- Previous 52-week high around ₹1,950–2,000.

In early 2026, price undercut the prior support and approached ₹1,020, then rapidly reclaimed the range. That failed breakdown or spring-like recovery was the first evidence that the long decline might be ending.

### 3. First low-risk entry below the 52-week high

The first entry area developed around ₹1,400–1,500:

- Price recovered strongly from approximately ₹1,020.
- It reclaimed the 200-day moving average.
- The 10/20/50-day averages turned upward.
- Price pulled back into the moving-average cluster without revisiting the base low.

### 10. Scanner research implications

The Wabag example suggests two separate templates rather than one over-broad screen.

**Template A — Early trend transition:**

- Strong recovery from a failed breakdown or major low.
- Reclaim of the 200-day average.
- Rising 10/20/50-day averages.
- Tight pullback near the short averages.
- Price still below but within practical distance of the 52-week high.
- Strong earnings and sales growth where point-in-time fundamentals are available.
- Compact stop and meaningful room to major resistance.

**Template B — Post-high continuation:**

- Price above the previous 52-week high.
- Mature bullish moving-average order.
- Prior high-volume thrust.
- One or more defended upside fair-value gaps.
- Tight high-level shelf near the rising 10-day average.
- Contracting volume during the shelf.
- Structural risk within approximately 4%–6%.

### 11. Case-study conclusion

Wabag shows why a scanner should distinguish early recovery entries from post-breakout continuation entries. The first setup captured trend transition below the old high with superior reward/risk. The second setup appeared only after the old high was cleared and offered stronger confirmation through price discovery, bullish moving-average alignment, volume-backed thrust, and renewed tightness.

The objective is not to buy merely because a stock is above its 52-week high. The desired second entry is a stock that breaks the high decisively, retains the advance, contracts near the rising 10-day average, shows quieter volume, and offers a nearby structural invalidation point.
